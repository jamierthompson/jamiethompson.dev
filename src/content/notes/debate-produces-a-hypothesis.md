---
title: "A debate produces a hypothesis rather than a decision"
description: "Adversarial debate sharpens reasoning without testing its inputs. If a crux claim is testable, stop and measure it — then keep the reasoning that lost."
pubDate: 2026-08-07
---

Independent drafting plus adversarial cross-examination produces a **hypothesis**. The decision
comes after someone measures.

Debate is a superb way to enumerate options and kill weak arguments. It is worthless against a
false premise every participant shares — the lenses inherit it and sharpen their reasoning on top
of it. Four capable agents cross-examining each other will not overturn a claim nobody thought to
check, because debate tests arguments, not inputs. So when a load-bearing claim is
runtime-testable, the highest-value move is to stop the argument and run the control — before the
debate converges, not after. Predictions are cheap; controls are what reverse verdicts. Before anything
LLM-shaped merges, the control is [[measure-dont-vibe-check|a baseline that has never seen it]].

The corollary: when a measurement overturns the verdict, **preserve the reasoning that lost**
rather than rewriting the record to match the outcome. It was sound on its premises, and the next
person to ask the question needs to know it was already argued well and still came out wrong —
otherwise they re-run the same debate and reach the same doomed verdict.

in [Four AI agents debated to a verdict and one measurement overturned it](/essays/the-reversal/).
